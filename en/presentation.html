Problem

Small businesses know they need to be on social media. But they fall into five traps

1

No time

The owner is busy with operations. Social media is always the “last thing to do”, month after month.

2

No skill

It is unclear what to write, about what, and in what tone. An empty editor is scarier than being busy.

3

No budget

An external team or an in-house marketer — $800 to $3,000 a month with unmanageable returns.

4

“Uninspiring” without a designer

Generic stock images you would be embarrassed to publish. The brand is unrecognizable in the feed.

5

Inconsistency

They run it for two weeks and stop. Consistency is the main driver of reach — and the main killer.

→

Consequence

The social presence is random, not systematic. A consistent competitor calmly takes the clients.

Moderation before publishing

Product · core

Brand DNA — the brand profile that works in every post

This is not a “company description” but a structured, versioned contract: text generation, Designer and moderation all follow it. The user can edit any element — manually or in chat.

  • Palette from the site’s CSS and visuals + a couple of fonts
  • Tone of voice on scales, archetype, “how we speak / how we don’t”
  • 3 audience segments with pains and triggers, up to 5 USPs
  • Key and off-limits topics — guardrails for regulated industries
  • Self-check by a second model: prices, addresses, services are verified against the site
≥ 90%

Target Brand DNA quality with no critical hallucinations on a control sample of 30 sites across industries — measured, not eyeballed.

Visibility is part of the value

The user sees step-by-step progress: “Reading the site → Analyzing the industry → Building Brand DNA → Writing the first posts”. The system is working — and it shows.

No website? There is a questionnaire

8 questions + a logo, optionally a link to a social profile. The product also works for offline businesses at the start.

Positioning

Not a tool and not a generator — an employee

Category: AI social media employee. Three pillars, defended in every product decision — they create the depth that cannot be copied over a weekend.

01

Brand DNA with visual identity

The style is extracted from the client’s own site — palette, fonts, voice, audience. The content sounds like you, not like a robot. Plus a fact self-check.

02

Layered visuals, not a picture

A template library + Designer with a single layer schema. The visuals are in the client’s colors and fonts, edited layer by layer, adapted to every format.

03

The feedback loop as an asset

Every action changes the next generation. The accumulated data is an in-house ML asset that improves with every client.

What we consciously are not

Yet another text generator, a cheap tool with manual idea input, or a “wrapper” with template output. The investment goes into depth: rendering, scheduling, integrations, self-learning.

Proof in the product

The core marketing move is a before/after on the client’s real site: a screenshot of the site → 6 posts in their colors. The honest answer to “show us” — we show it immediately, before sign-up.

Model sensitivity

  • AI prices +30% → margin 93% → 92% — immaterial
  • CAC $250 instead of $120 → LTV/CAC ≈ 6 — growth costs $8–10k/mo more
  • Churn 10% instead of 7% → LTV −30%, month 12 — 620 clients instead of 800
  • Trial → paid conversion 15% instead of 25% → breakeven shifts by +3–4 months, +$50k needed

Investor takeaway

The model is resilient to AI prices and CAC growth. It is sensitive to two things — conversion to paid and retention. That is why the investment goes into generation quality, visuals and the feedback loop.
